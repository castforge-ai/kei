Find and vet references. Write a REFS.md table (Ref, Tier, What it gives, Our checks, Verdict) with 3 vetted rows or
more, each with a tier (R, A, B or C) and a verdict (keep, flag or reject). Add to PLAN.md a "Refs:" line naming the
REFS.md files and a "Short look:" line saying what you measured in the clips. References are studied and measured,
never traced or copied.

When the shot has refs/mocap/<name>/ (motion capture the author imported with kei ref import), it is a measured
reference you may use: read its measures.json (speed, pelvis bob, contact frames, kei check numbers) and its
sheet.png, add its REF_ROW.md row to REFS.md, and measure against it in the short look. Never copy its joints into
keys: the plan and the keys are yours.

Cite each reference by its authors, year and title when it is published research, a dataset or recorded footage. A
craft reference is never named, by title or by author: its row says what it shows in your own words, with the tier B.
Every row is your own words: quote at most one sentence of a source.

Numbers: write (unchecked) right after a number that states a fact about the real world when you did not read it in
the source itself during this shot, such as a value you remember or one taken from a summary: "knee about 5 deg
(unchecked) at contact". A number you read and measured in the source carries no mark, and so does every number of
the shot itself (frames, key values, the timings you chose, what Kei's tools measured). Kei counts the marks. The author sees the marks on the live page, and a number stays unchecked until
someone reads it in the source.
